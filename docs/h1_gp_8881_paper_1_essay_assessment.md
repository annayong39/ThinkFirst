## Exam facts

- **Task:** Choose 1 of 8 essay questions and write **500–800 words**.
- **Time:** **1 hour 30 minutes**.
- **Marks:** **50 total** — Content **30**, Language **20**.
- **Weighting:** **50%** of the overall examination.
- Questions address issues of local, regional and global significance. They are general in nature and call for knowledge across disciplines and conceptual understanding.

## Content — 30 marks

| Band | Marks | Concise paraphrase of criteria | Within-band guidance |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **5** | **25–30** | Defines the question precisely; sustains a relevant, insightful and balanced argument; explains conceptual links, trends and relationships; develops wide-ranging examples; evaluates different perspectives and contexts; reaches a measured conclusion. | **28–30:** examples consistently evaluated, nuanced observations and fully explained links. **25–27:** examples mostly evaluated; links explained but less fully developed. |
| **4** | **19–24** | Understands and defines the question; gives a largely relevant, detailed and balanced discussion with frequent suitable examples, some conceptual links and evaluation; reaches a supported conclusion. | **22–24:** wide-ranging examples, clear links and frequent evaluation. **19–21:** some links and evaluative comments. |
| **3** | **13–18** | Generally understands and mostly addresses the question; shows occasional or implicit conceptual understanding; uses suitable but narrow or underdeveloped examples; attempts balance and some analysis; conclusion may mainly summarise or assert. | **16–18:** some analysis and implied links. **13–15:** question-focused but more generalised and underdeveloped. |
| **2** | **7–12** | Partly understands the question, often discussing its broad topic; shows limited conceptual links, relevance, analysis and balance; examples are few, weakly developed or unevenly relevant; conclusion has little support. | **10–12:** attempts an argument and some developed examples, with uneven coherence. **7–9:** partial argument and/or undeveloped examples. |
| **1** | **1–6** | Misunderstands the question; offers little relevant, coherent argument, conceptual understanding, analysis or supporting illustration; conclusion may be absent or merely an opinion. | **4–6:** occasional ideas related to the wider topic. **1–3:** little relevant content. |

**0 marks:** Nothing in the response meets any Content criterion.

### What Content marking looks for

- Accurate understanding of the **specific terms and scope** of the question, with sustained relevance.
- A clear, cogent argument supported by **appropriate, developed examples**.
- Analysis and evaluation of issues, differing perspectives and relevant contexts; balance where appropriate.
- **Conceptual understanding:** observations about trends and relationships, explained connections across ideas, and application to other contexts where relevant.
- A conclusion supported by the discussion and appropriately measured.

## Language — 20 marks

| Band | Marks | Concise paraphrase of criteria |
|---|---:|---|
| **5** | **17–20** | Assured expression and organisation; very few errors that do not obstruct meaning; varied, complex sentences; sophisticated, apt vocabulary; coherent paragraphs and logical links. |
| **4** | **13–16** | Controlled, clear expression; few serious errors; varied sentences and appropriate vocabulary; effective paragraphs and links. |
| **3** | **9–12** | Generally competent expression; errors may be frequent but do not significantly obstruct meaning; some attempted variety; mostly apt vocabulary; basic sequencing and links, sometimes repetitive. |
| **2** | **5–8** | Frequent errors occasionally obstruct meaning; limited sentence and vocabulary range; basic paragraphing and links; ideas can be difficult to follow and may require rereading. |
| **1** | **1–4** | Persistent errors regularly obstruct meaning; little sentence control, basic or misused vocabulary, and weak organisation; substantial rereading may be needed. |

**0 marks:** Nothing in the response meets any Language criterion.

**Language is judged through:** spelling, punctuation and grammar; sentence control and variety; vocabulary range and accuracy; paragraphing, linking and logical sequencing.

## Additional Content guidance

- **“Your society” / specified society:** Anchor the answer in a particular society. A “your society” answer may use the candidate's society outside Singapore; if the question specifies Singapore, focus on Singapore. Comparisons with other societies can help, provided the primary focus stays on the society required by the question.
- **Conceptual understanding:** Notice trends, relationships and connections across issues and ideas; apply or adapt ideas to other times or places when relevant; analyse and evaluate local, regional and global issues and their implications for individuals and society where relevant.
